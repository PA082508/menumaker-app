# Income categories — two doors

A child's income category — **Free, Reduced or Paid** — can be set in two places.
They are not two systems and not two sets of rules. They are two doors into the same
record, and which one you use depends on **what is in front of you**.

## Door 1 — a stack of papers → Paper applications

You have a pile of signed income applications from the safe, and you are working
through it.

Open **Paper applications** (`/iea-confirm`). One line is one **household**: the
application covers the whole family, so one entry writes the record for every active
child in it — you do not open three cards for three brothers.

The list holds only the children an application is actually waiting for: **Free or
Reduced with nothing on file**. A Paid child is not there, because Paid is not a
determination — it is the absence of one. A child whose paper is already filed is not
there either. If you search for such a child, the screen names them and says why they
are not in the list, so you never have to wonder whether the record was lost.

For each family you enter the **category printed on the application**, the **date
printed on it** — not today's date — and confirm the paper is filed. Nothing is
written until you press Confirm.

## Door 2 — one child, one event → the child's card

A single family walks in with one form, or one child's category changes mid-year.
Open the child's card and set the category there, together with the document date.

The card is also where you see **where a value came from**: the provenance panel
carries "Where this change comes from" and a full **Change history** — who entered it,
when, from which document and with what date. A category without that trail is a number
nobody can defend at an inspection.

## The rules are the same behind both doors

- **Free and Reduced always need a document date** — the date on the paper. Without it
  the category cannot be counted in the claim, and both doors refuse it.
- **Paid needs no application.** It is the state "no determination was made".
- **One application, one household.** Whichever door you use, the determination belongs
  to the family, not to a single child.
- **Nothing is applied silently.** Every write lands in the change history with its
  source, and a refusal is always explained in words.

## Who sees this

Income is organisation-level work. A centre director does not see income determinations
at all — not the applications, not the amounts, and not a counter for them. On their
screens a child simply carries a content-free "determination on file" marker. That is
deliberate: the category tells you what a family earns, and that is not information a
centre needs in order to feed a child.
